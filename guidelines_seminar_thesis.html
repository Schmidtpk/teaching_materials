Guidelines for Seminar Papers and Theses

Word Count (Main Text Only, excluding Title and References):

  • Seminar Paper Master: maximum 5000 words (or 40,000 characters)
  • Seminar Paper Bachelor: maximum 3500 words (or 30,000 characters)

Scope:

  • Maximum values above, you may submit shorter papers (Quality over quantity)
  • Try to express your findings as precisely as possible
  • Avoid general text; instead, refer to sources
  • Tip: At the end of the process, the seminar paper should be shortened

Content & Argumentation

  • Precise Research Question: Formulate a research question and align the content accordingly.
  • No “Crime Thriller Effect”: The result/thesis belongs in the introduction. Do not aim for entertainment value.
  • Literature: Literature work should compare, structure, and critically evaluate (Synthesis instead of listing)

Literature & Source Work

  • Quality over Quantity: Primarily use Peer-Reviewed Journals (e.g., American Economic Review, JPE, QJE) and current Working Papers (NBER, etc).
  • Textbooks & Internet: Use textbooks only for basic definitions. Internet sources (Statista, Wikipedia, Blogs) are only permissible for data/facts (not for theory).
  • Original Sources: Cite the source (“Keynes, 1936”), not the textbook that writes about Keynes (“Mankiw, 2020”).
  • Language Models are not a valid source.

Academic Style

  • References: If not your own work, name the source. Caution with AI output: Often uses original texts without naming the source (or names a wrong source).
  • Objectivity: Write precisely and objectively.
  • Readability: Write as easily understandable as possible. Avoid complicated sentence structures.
  • Definitions: Central terms must be clearly defined upon their first occurrence.
  • Consistency: Notation, citations, tables, and titles should be consistent. For formula notations, this often means that the original formulas cannot be simply adopted, but various sources must be combined.

Formal Correctness

  • Consistent Citation: uniform throughout the entire document (text & bibliography)
  • Figures & Tables: These must be self-explanatory (“Stand-alone”). Every graphic needs a number, a title, and a source if not self-created (e.g., “Source: Own illustration based on Müller (2019)”).
  • Variables: Every mathematical variable must be explained in the text (“where \(Y\) stands for GDP”).

Software

  • You can use software of your choice.
  • Submissions as paper and pdf
  • Possible Solution: MSWord, Google Docs
    • Easy
    • Not perfect for formulas, tables, and large papers
    • Literature management is possible with Zotero
  • Possible Solution: Latex + bibtex
    • Standard for Academic Work
    • Requires more effort to learn than Word/Docs, etc.
    • Easiest: Cloud Solution (e.g., Overleaf)
      • Example at https://www.overleaf.com/read/fvrszjkqdbvc#caebdd
  • Possible Solution: Quarto in RStudio
    • More convenient and more possibilities than Latex
    • Can directly include code
    • But more complex if errors occur
    • Example at https://github.com/Schmidtpk/materials/tree/main/thesis%20skelett%20quarto
